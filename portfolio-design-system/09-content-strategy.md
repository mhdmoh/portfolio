# 09 — Content Strategy

## Role of content in the product

Content is the product. Visual design exists to stage a **professional narrative** and make proof scannable. The voice should transfer even when specific jobs, degrees, and projects change for a new career stage.

---

## Tone of voice

| Dimension | Guidance |
|-----------|----------|
| Person | First person in narrative (`I embarked…`); third-person omitted |
| Formality | Professional, lightly formal; not academic stiff, not casual meme-y |
| Confidence | Assertive about craft (`Expert in…`, leadership verbs) without bombast |
| Emotion | Restrained warmth; curiosity and growth appear as values |
| Humor | Minimal in chrome; product descriptions may inherit app marketing playfulness |
| Jargon | Comfortable with engineering terms in tags and descriptions; explain outcomes in prose |

**Voice one-liner:** *A senior mobile engineer speaking plainly about what he built, led, and learned.*

---

## Writing style

### Hero / identity copy

- **Name:** plain legal/professional name, no nickname lockup required.  
- **Profession:** specific title string, not a vague “Creator” / “Problem Solver.”  
- **Summary:** one or two short lines focusing on outcomes + craft (performance, clean code, UI/UX). Line break allowed for rhythm.

### Cover letter

- Short origin story with a year marker.  
- Enumerate contexts (agency, startup, freelance, leadership) to show range.  
- End on a growth principle (continuous learning / adaptability).  
- No call-to-action soft close (“Let’s chat!”) inside the letter—CTAs live in identity chrome.

### Experience descriptions

- Start with strong verbs: *Spearheaded, Lead, Optimize, Mentor, Integrate.*  
- Mix delivery (shipped apps) with quality systems (CI/CD, architecture, mentoring).  
- One compact paragraph per role (not bullet novellas).  
- Tags carry the stack vocabulary so prose can stay readable.

### Education

- Degree name as primary claim.  
- Institution as supporting line.  
- Date honesty including `PRESENT`.

### Articles

- Title matches public post.  
- Summary explains **problem + approach + who benefits**, in 2–4 sentences.  
- Topics are concrete technologies/concepts.

### Projects

- Lead with what the product does for users.  
- Consumer apps may use lighter marketing tone; enterprise apps stay plain.  
- Technical tags spotlight differentiators (biometric auth, Leanback TV, sockets, etc.).  
- Prefer outcomes and capabilities over internal ticket lore.

---

## Section objectives

| Section | Objective | Reader question answered |
|---------|-----------|----------------------------|
| Identity | Name the offer and open a channel | Who are you and how do I reach you? |
| About | Provide human context and arc | What’s your story? |
| Experience | Prove seniority and fit | Where have you done the work? |
| Education | Formal credibility / trajectory | What’s your foundation / current growth? |
| Articles | Show thinking and communication | Can you explain and teach? |
| Projects | Show artifacts in the wild | What exists because of you? |

---

## Content hierarchy (messaging priorities)

1. **Who + role**  
2. **How to contact**  
3. **Core craft promise** (summary)  
4. **Career arc** (about)  
5. **Most recent / senior experience**  
6. **Education**  
7. **Public writing**  
8. **Product portfolio**  
9. **Fine-grained tags** (scanned, not deeply read)

Anything that doesn’t serve these priorities (quotes, hobby galleries, long blogs on-page) is secondary.

---

## Storytelling approach

The portfolio tells a **linear professional bildungsroman**:

1. **Exposition:** identity + craft promise  
2. **Backstory:** about (2019 → multi-context experience)  
3. **Rising action:** roles with increasing responsibility  
4. **Credentials beat:** education, including ongoing master’s  
5. **Voice beat:** articles  
6. **Climax of proof:** shipped projects across domains (social/map, health, banking, HR, IPTV, ERP, mobility)

It is **chronology-aware** but not a diary. Time appears as date ranges; meaning appears as verbs and tags.

### Narrative themes to preserve across content updates

- Mobility / cross-platform craft  
- End-to-end ownership  
- Leadership and standards  
- Curiosity and continuous learning  
- Real-world deployment (stores, production systems)

---

## Messaging do’s and don’ts

### Do

- Be specific (Flutter, CI/CD, mentoring, store links).  
- Keep paragraphs short.  
- Align titles with reality (no title inflation).  
- Reuse vocabulary between jobs and project tags for coherence.

### Don’t

- Lead with soft slogans (“Turning caffeine into code”).  
- Overwhelm with emoji in professional sections (project consumer copy may differ).  
- Hide gaps with vague date ranges.  
- Duplicate the same paragraph across every job.  
- Use the about section as a keyword stuffing block.

---

## Localization and personal details

- Location presented as `City, Country`.  
- Phone in locally readable formatting with country code available to dialers.  
- External profiles use stable public URLs.  
- Document language is **English** (international hiring audience).

---

## Content maintenance guidelines

When updating for a new career stage:

1. Rewrite **Profession** and **Summary** first—they set positioning.  
2. Refresh **About** to match the new arc (without discarding humility/growth).  
3. Reorder or rewrite **Experience** for relevance, not vanity completeness.  
4. Keep **Projects** curated; fewer strong case items beat a long weak list.  
5. Retire tags that no longer support the target role.  
6. Ensure every outbound link still resolves.

Visual identity can stay stable while copy pivots—this is expected.
