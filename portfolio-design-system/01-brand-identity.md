# 01 — Brand Identity

## Overall personality and tone

The portfolio presents a **calm, senior, engineering-first professional**. It feels closer to a carefully typeset résumé in a dark studio than to a marketing landing page.

| Attribute | Expression |
|-----------|------------|
| **Personality** | Confident, precise, quietly ambitious, technically grounded |
| **Tone** | Professional, direct, slightly formal without being stiff |
| **Energy** | Low-to-medium: measured motion, dark canvas, sparse accents |
| **Humor** | Almost none in chrome; occasional warmth in project copy (e.g., app product language) |
| **Attitude toward self** | Capability-led, not hype-led; achievements shown as evidence, not slogans |

The brand does **not** try to be playful, maximalist, startup-neon, or “creative agency” flashy. It tries to feel like someone who ships mobile products and leads teams—someone a hiring manager or founder can trust after a short scroll.

---

## Design philosophy

1. **Identity before spectacle.** The person’s name and role own the first impression. Decorative systems (pattern, glow) support atmosphere; they never replace content.
2. **Proof over pitch.** After a short narrative, the site becomes a sequence of verifiable artifacts: employers, degrees, articles, apps.
3. **Restraint as craft signal.** Limited palette, default-system typography, subtle hovers, and generous dark space communicate taste and seniority more than ornamental UI does.
4. **Desktop as workspace, mobile as story.** Large screens keep the identity “pinned” like a business card beside a document. Small screens tell the same story top-to-bottom without competing chrome.
5. **Interaction as confirmation.** Hover and motion acknowledge attention; they do not invent a game or a branded animation language.

---

## Emotional goals

What the visitor should feel within the first few seconds:

| Goal | How it is achieved |
|------|--------------------|
| **Competence** | Senior title, dense skill tags, shipped app screenshots, leadership language |
| **Calm trust** | Dark green field, soft accents, no aggressive CTAs or popups |
| **Approachability** | Visible phone number, location, and social icons—human and reachable |
| **Curiosity** | Hover highlights and external-link arrows invite exploration of jobs/articles/apps |
| **Continuity / growth** | Narrative about starting in 2019, agency → startup → freelance → leadership, plus ongoing study |

What the visitor should **not** feel: urgency, sales pressure, confusion about who this is for, or sensory overload.

---

## Target audience

Primary audiences (in approximate priority for this product stage):

1. **Technical hiring managers / tech leads** evaluating a senior mobile engineer or team lead.
2. **Founders / product owners** seeking a Flutter / cross-platform mobile contractor or hire.
3. **Peer engineers** who arrive via GitHub, Medium, or referrals and want a quick credibility check.
4. **Secondary:** university / network contacts; not optimized for consumer brand discovery.

Implied visitor context: short attention, high skepticism of fluff, preference for scannable timelines and tech stack signals.

## First impressions

### Desktop (wide viewport)

1. Deep forest-green environment with a faint technical pattern and a soft green glow that may move with the cursor.
2. Left side: large white name, role, two-line value proposition, divider, location, phone.
3. Middle of left column: vertical section navigation with an expanding underline indicator.
4. Bottom of left column: GitHub / LinkedIn / Medium icons.
5. Right side: grey narrative paragraph (“cover letter”), then experience cards waiting below the fold or just entering view.

The first viewport reads as **one composition**: identity card + opening narrative, not a dashboard of widgets.

### Mobile / narrow viewport

1. Same dark environment.
2. Identity block stacked at the top (name → role → summary → location → phone → socials).
3. Cover letter follows.
4. Explicit section headings introduce Experience, Education, Articles, Projects as the user scrolls.

No hamburger menu. No multi-page chrome. The “site” is a single continuous document.

## What the design communicates

| Message | Signal |
|---------|--------|
| “I am a senior mobile specialist” | Title, Flutter/native tags, app store links, project thumbnails |
| “I lead and ship” | Leadership titles, end-to-end product language, CI/CD and architecture tags |
| “I am serious but not cold” | Soft green glow, rounded tags, readable spacing, reachable phone |
| “I care about craft” | Hover polish, consistent card structure, restrained motion |
| “I am currently in Europe / Budapest” | Explicit location line |
| “I write and teach as well as build” | Articles section placed before projects in the scroll order |

---

## What makes it memorable

1. **The forest-green atmosphere** — unusual compared with default black/purple developer portfolios; feels botanical-technical rather than cyberpunk.
2. **The sticky identity column** — the person remains present while evidence scrolls past (desktop).
3. **The ambient cursor glow** — a quiet “living” quality without animation noise.
4. **The pattern veil** — mobile-device and tooling line icons at near-invisible opacity, thematically on-brand for a mobile engineer.
5. **Consistent external-link arrow language** across jobs, education, articles, and projects.
6. **Green skill pills** that create a rhythmic visual signature in every experience/project block.

---

## Brand do’s and don’ts (for recreation)

### Do

- Keep the environment dark and green-dominant.
- Lead with name + role + one crisp value proposition.
- Prefer evidence lists over marketing sections (“Services”, “Testimonials carousels”, etc.) unless the new career stage requires them.
- Keep accent color reserved for interactive feedback and metadata chips.
- Maintain a sense of quiet professionalism.

### Don’t

- Replace the calm field with loud gradients, glassmorphism stacks, or purple/indigo AI-default themes.
- Overpower the name with a clever headline.
- Add sticky banners, cookie-style distraction, or autoplaying media.
- Turn hover into bounce / elastic novelty.
- Hide contact information behind forms if direct contact is part of the identity.
